Chronological Timeline

The major theoretical and engineering breakthroughs that transformed how machines understand human language.

1957 – 1975 Era I · Lexical IR H.P. Luhn (IBM), Karen Spärck Jones (Cambridge), Gerard Salton (Cornell)
Foundations of Information Retrieval: TF, IDF & the Vector Space Model

Before semantic search, early search engines operated on Boolean logic. In 1957, Hans Peter Luhn introduced term frequency (TF) for automatic document indexing. In 1972, Karen Spärck Jones introduced Inverse Document Frequency (IDF), establishing that rare words carry more semantic specificity than frequent words.

In 1975, Gerard Salton synthesized these into the Vector Space Model (VSM) in the SMART retrieval system, representing documents as high-dimensional sparse vectors indexed by vocabulary words.

$$\text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \log\left(\frac{N}{|\{d \in D : t \in d\}|}\right)$$
◆
Historical Significance: Established term frequency and specificity as mathematical vectors, enabling ranked retrieval via Cosine Similarity for the first time.
1994 Era I · Lexical IR Stephen Robertson & Karen Spärck Jones (TREC-3)
Okapi BM25: The Gold Standard of Lexical Ranking

Introduced during the TREC conferences, Okapi BM25 refined TF-IDF by adding non-linear term frequency saturation and document length normalization, preventing long verbose documents from unfairly dominating search results.

$$\text{Score}_{\text{BM25}}(D, Q) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{IDF}(q_i) \cdot \frac{f(q_i, D) \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{f(q_i, D) + k_1 \cdot \left(1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}$$
▲
Fundamental Limitation: BM25 suffers from the Vocabulary Mismatch Problem. If a query asks for "car repair" and a document contains "automobile maintenance", BM25 scores a 0 match because tokens do not overlap exact strings.
1990 – 2003 Era II · Latent Semantics Deerwester et al. (1990), Thomas Hofmann (1999), Blei, Ng, Jordan (2003)
Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI/LSA) & Topic Modeling

To overcome vocabulary mismatch, Scott Deerwester, Susan Dumais et al. (1990) invented Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI). Using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), LSI factored a huge, sparse term-document matrix \(X\) into lower-rank dense matrices, uncovering latent concepts shared by words.

This was followed by pLSA (1999) and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA, 2003), which modeled documents as probabilistic mixtures of latent topics.

$$X_{m \times n} \approx U_{m \times k} \, \Sigma_{k \times k} \, V_{n \times k}^T$$
◆
The Paradigm Shift: First true semantic representation! Words like "automobile" and "car" mapped to nearby coordinates in the latent concept space even if they didn't appear together in the same document.
2013 – 2014 Era III · Neural Vectors Tomas Mikolov et al. (Google 2013), Jeffrey Pennington et al. (Stanford 2014)
Word2Vec & GloVe: Dense Neural Word Embeddings

Tomas Mikolov et al. introduced Word2Vec (Continuous Bag-of-Words and Skip-Gram models), training 2-layer neural networks on massive text corpora to predict word contexts. In 2014, Stanford introduced GloVe using global matrix factorization.

These models mapped words to continuous dense vectors (e.g. 300 dimensions) where spatial distances captured complex semantic relationships and linear vector arithmetic.

$$\vec{v}_{\text{King}} - \vec{v}_{\text{Man}} + \vec{v}_{\text{Woman}} \approx \vec{v}_{\text{Queen}}$$
▲
Limitation: Static word vectors assigned a single static vector to each word string. "Bank" had identical representations whether referring to a river bank or a financial bank.
2016 – 2017 Era III · Neural Vectors Malkov & Yashunin (HNSW, 2016), Johnson et al. (FAISS, Facebook AI 2017)
Scale Revolution: HNSW & FAISS Vector Indexing

Searching millions of high-dimensional vectors sequentially takes \(O(N \cdot d)\) time, which is far too slow for real-time search. In 2016, Malkov & Yashunin created HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World graphs), enabling sub-linear \(O(\log N)\) approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) vector search.

In 2017, Facebook AI released FAISS, GPU-accelerating vector similarity search over billions of vectors using Product Quantization (PQ) and Inverted File (IVF) indexes.

◆
Engineering Impact: Unlocked millisecond vector retrieval speeds over billion-scale embedding spaces, laying the foundation for modern Vector Databases.
2017 – 2018 Era IV · Transformers Vaswani et al. (2017), Jacob Devlin et al. (Google AI 2018)
Transformers & BERT: Contextual Neural Encoders

The Transformer architecture ("Attention Is All You Need") introduced multi-head self-attention. In 2018, Google introduced BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers).

Unlike static Word2Vec vectors, BERT computes context-dependent embeddings for every word in a sequence. The word "bank" receives distinct vector representations based on surrounding context ("river bank" vs. "bank account").

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$
◆
Breakthrough: Completely solved polysemy and complex sentence semantics, elevating search relevance across Google Search (2019 rollout).
2019 – 2020 Era IV · Transformers Reimers & Gurevych (SBERT, 2019), Karpukhin et al. (Meta DPR, 2020), Khattab & Zaharia (ColBERT, Stanford 2020)
Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) & Late Interaction (ColBERT)

Standard BERT requires feeding every (query, passage) pair together through all transformer layers, which was far too slow for full-corpus search. Sentence-BERT (SBERT) and Dense Passage Retriever (DPR) introduced Bi-Encoder architectures, independently embedding queries and passages into single dense vectors for instant MIPS (Maximum Inner Product Search).

In 2020, Omar Khattab & Matei Zaharia created ColBERT (Contextualized Late Interaction over BERT), preserving token-level contextual interactions via a fast \(\text{MaxSim}\) operator while allowing offline document index pre-computation.

$$\text{Score}_{\text{ColBERT}}(q, d) = \sum_{i \in |E_q|} \max_{j \in |E_d|} \left( E_{q_i} \cdot E_{d_j}^T \right)$$
◆
Architecture Evolution: Bi-Encoders enabled end-to-end vector search, while ColBERT's Late Interaction bridged the gap between bi-encoder speed and cross-encoder precision.
2020 – 2023 Era V · RAG & Vector DBs Patrick Lewis et al. (Meta AI / UCL, 2020)
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) & Vector Databases

In 2020, Patrick Lewis et al. introduced RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), uniting dense retriever non-parametric memory (DPR index) with a generative seq2seq transformer (BART / LLM parametric memory).

This triggered the explosion of dedicated Vector Databases (Pinecone, Qdrant, Milvus, Weaviate, Chroma, pgvector), designed to store, index, filter, and retrieve high-dimensional embedding vectors at production scale.

◆
Modern Standard: Grounding Large Language Models in external enterprise data, dramatically reducing hallucinations and providing real-time knowledge updates.
2023 – Present Era V · Modern Production Modern Search Engineering Standards
Hybrid Search (BM25 + Dense Vectors) & Two-Stage Re-Ranking

Pure vector search has weaknesses: exact keyword matching for serial numbers, part IDs, rare technical acronyms, or specific proper nouns can fail. Modern production search architectures employ Hybrid Search, merging BM25 sparse lexical scores with Dense Vector semantic embeddings using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF).

They follow a Two-Stage Architecture: Stage 1 retrieves top ~100-1000 candidate documents via fast Hybrid Search; Stage 2 applies a heavy Cross-Encoder Re-Ranker (Cohere Rerank, BGE-Reranker) to output hyper-accurate top-5 rankings.

$$\text{RRF Score}(d \in D) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{1}{k + r_m(d)}$$
◆
State of the Art: Combines the precision of 1970s lexical indexing with the semantic depth of 2020s Transformer contextual embeddings.
